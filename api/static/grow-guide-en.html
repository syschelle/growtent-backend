Cannabis Cultivation Guide – From Seed or Cutting to Drying

Version: 1.0
As of: November 2026
Scope: Indoor cultivation under LED lighting
Varieties: Strain-independent, photoperiod plants and autoflowers

1. Fundamentals

This guide describes the complete life cycle of a cannabis plant:

  1. Germination or rooting cuttings
  2. Seedling and young-plant stage
  3. Early vegetative stage
  4. Late vegetative stage
  5. Flowering onset and stretch
  6. Early flowering
  7. Mid flowering
  8. Late flowering and maturation
  9. Harvest
  10. Drying
  11. Optional: curing

All climate and VPD values are guidance ranges. Genetics, plant condition, lighting, substrate and root development may require adjustments.

These time frames are only for planning. Actual plant development is what matters.

2. VPD – Vapor Pressure Deficit

Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD) describes the difference between the saturation vapor pressure at an evaporating surface and the actual water-vapor pressure of the surrounding air.

The unit is kilopascal (kPa).

  • Low VPD: Little evaporative demand; usually a more humid environment.
  • Moderate VPD: Balanced water movement and transpiration.
  • High VPD: High evaporative demand; exceeding the plant's capacity risks water stress.

2.1 Air VPD and leaf VPD

Two calculation methods must be distinguished.

Air VPD:

Calculated solely from air temperature and relative humidity.

Leaf VPD:

Also accounts for leaf-surface temperature.

Under LED lighting, leaf surfaces may, for example, be 1–3 °C cooler than the surrounding air. However, this difference is not constant and should preferably be measured.

Example:

  • Air temperature: 25 °C
  • Relative humidity: 60%
  • Leaf-surface temperature: 23 °C
  • Air VPD: approx. 1.27 kPa
  • Leaf VPD: approx. 0.91 kPa

Important: These values are not interchangeable. Where possible, the following VPD recommendations refer to leaf VPD. If using a leaf-temperature offset, keep it consistent across stages and as realistic as possible.

2.2 VPD reference table for the full grow

The following table is a practical, strain-independent stage plan. The finer subdivision of flowering is guidance within broader published reference ranges.

These VPD ranges are guidance for the light period.

Minor occasional deviations are generally less problematic than persistently unsuitable conditions. High VPD should not be aggravated by excessive drying of the substrate.

Special note for late flowering: Lower temperatures may reduce calculated VPD. Do not reduce relative humidity at any cost just to reach 1.5 kPa. Mold prevention and a tolerable plant temperature take priority.

3.1 LED light intensity

PPFD refers to the amount of photosynthetically active light per square meter per second.

Development stage Typical PPFD range
Seedling 100–300 µmol/m²/s
Unrooted cutting 100–250 µmol/m²/s
Vegetative stage 300–600 µmol/m²/s
Flowering – early 500–700 µmol/m²/s
Flowering – mid/late 600–900 µmol/m²/s

These are conservative guidance ranges for LED cultivation without supplemental CO₂ enrichment.

More light is not automatically better. The suitable level also depends on daily light integral (DLI), lighting duration and the plant's health.

4. Stage 1A – Seed germination

Duration: Usually 1–7 days, occasionally longer.

Goal

Develop a healthy taproot and a stable seedling.

Conditions

  • Temperature: 22–25 °C
  • Growing medium: Evenly moist, not wet
  • Light: Not required for germination itself
  • VPD: Not yet relevant while no functional leaves are present

Procedure

  1. Prepare a suitable loose seed-starting medium and lightly moisten it.
  2. Insert the seed about 0.5–1 cm deep.
  3. Cover carefully with growing medium.
  4. Maintain consistent warmth and moisture.
  5. Avoid waterlogging.
  6. After the seedling emerges, provide gentle, suitable light.

Checks

  • Do not let the medium dry out completely.
  • Avoid standing water.
  • Avoid touching or damaging the taproot.
  • Gradually acclimatize the young plant to lighting after emergence.

Transition: The seedling stage begins when cotyledons have opened and the plant starts to develop true leaves.

5. Stage 1B – Cuttings

This stage applies to freshly taken, unrooted cuttings. A well-rooted cutting can move straight to the young-plant stage.

Rooting duration: Often 7–14 days.

Goal

Develop a functioning root system with as little water loss as possible.

Conditions

Parameter Recommendation
Air temperature 22–26 °C
Relative humidity 65–75%
Leaf VPD 0.4–0.8 kPa
VPD planning value 0.6 kPa
Lighting 18/6
Light intensity 100–250 PPFD

Procedure

  1. Choose a healthy shoot from a vegetatively growing mother plant.
  2. Take a cutting with a clean, disinfected tool.
  3. Remove lower leaves and prepare an appropriate cut surface.
  4. Place the cutting in a moist rooting medium.
  5. Use a transparent dome if needed, without keeping leaves permanently wet.
  6. Check daily for wilting, mold and new roots.
  7. After roots form, gradually reduce humidity over several days.

Checks

  • Cuttings should not remain wilted continuously.
  • Keep the rooting medium moist but aerated.
  • Air movement should be very gentle.
  • Avoid a strong fan blowing directly on them.
  • Avoid unnecessarily high light intensity.

Transition: Visible new roots and vigorous new leaf growth mark the start of the young-plant stage.

6. Stage 2 – Seedling and young plant

Duration: Often about 1–2 weeks after germination, depending on development.

Goal

Build the first true leaves and a viable root system.

Conditions

  • Day temperature: 22–25 °C
  • Night temperature: 20–22 °C
  • Relative humidity: 65–75%
  • VPD: 0.4–0.8 kPa
  • VPD planning value: 0.6 kPa
  • Lighting: 18/6
  • PPFD: 100–300 µmol/m²/s

Care

  • Use small amounts of water according to the size of the root ball.
  • Do not continuously saturate the whole pot.
  • Fertilize sparingly at first, particularly in pre-fertilized soil.
  • Watch for uniform, healthy leaf growth.
  • Transplant to a larger container when needed.
  • Maintain high humidity without stagnant condensation.

Typical problems

Long, thin stems: May indicate insufficient light.

Drooping leaves and constantly wet substrate: Possible overwatering.

Dry leaf edges: Possibly excessive light, high VPD or lack of water.

Transition: Several healthy pairs of true leaves, active roots and noticeably accelerating growth.

7. Stage 3 – Early vegetative stage

Duration: Often from week 2–3 after germination; for cuttings, after successful rooting.

Goal

Vigorous root, main-stem and side-branch growth.

Conditions

  • Day temperature: 23–27 °C
  • Night temperature: 20–23 °C
  • Relative humidity: 55–70%
  • VPD: 0.8–1.1 kPa
  • VPD planning value: 0.95 kPa
  • Lighting: 18/6
  • PPFD: 300–500 µmol/m²/s

Care

  • Match watering to actual consumption.
  • Allow sufficient drying and oxygen in the root zone before watering again.
  • Gradually adjust nutrients to increasing demand.
  • Ensure sufficient air circulation and fresh-air supply.
  • Aim for uniform illumination.
  • Regularly inspect for pests and unusual changes in leaves.

Signs of growth

  • New leaf pairs appear more quickly.
  • Side branches begin to develop.
  • Water and nutrient consumption increase.
  • Roots progressively colonize the entire substrate.

8. Stage 4 – Late vegetative stage

Duration: Freely controlled in photoperiod plants.

Goal

Build a stable plant structure and sufficient leaf area for subsequent flowering.

Conditions

  • Day temperature: 23–28 °C
  • Night temperature: 20–24 °C
  • Relative humidity: 50–65%
  • VPD: 0.9–1.2 kPa
  • VPD planning value: 1.1 kPa
  • Lighting: 18/6
  • PPFD: 400–600 µmol/m²/s

Care

  • Match watering to root volume.
  • Check nutrient supply against substrate and fertilizer plan.
  • Ensure even air circulation.
  • Remove damaged or diseased leaves.
  • Optionally use gentle low-stress training.
  • Leave enough space for the subsequent stretch.

Preparing for flowering

Photoperiod plants are induced to flower by a consistent dark period of about 12 hours.

Plants can gain considerable height in the first flowering weeks. Check lighting and available space before switching.

Autoflowers enter flowering independently of changes to the light cycle.

9. Stage 5 – Flowering onset and stretch

Flowering weeks: 1–2

For photoperiod plants, week counting starts with the change to 12/12. For autoflowers, use the visible onset of flowering.

Goal

A healthy transition from vegetative growth to flower formation.

Conditions

  • Day temperature: 23–27 °C
  • Night temperature: 19–23 °C
  • Relative humidity: 50–60%
  • VPD: 1.0–1.2 kPa
  • VPD planning value: 1.1 kPa
  • Photoperiod: 12/12
  • Autoflower: usually 18/6
  • PPFD: approx. 500–700 µmol/m²/s

Development

  • Strong elongation of main stems and side branches.
  • First visible flower sites.
  • Increasing demand for water and nutrients.
  • Changes in growth rate.

Care

  • Check light distance regularly.
  • Avoid abrupt overfeeding when switching to bloom nutrients.
  • Provide good airflow.
  • Avoid extreme temperature fluctuations.
  • Do not interrupt the dark period of photoperiod plants with stray light.

10. Stage 6 – Early flowering

Flowering weeks: 3–4

Goal

Healthy inflorescences and stable side branches.

Conditions

  • Day temperature: 22–26 °C
  • Night temperature: 18–22 °C
  • Relative humidity: 45–55%
  • VPD: 1.1–1.4 kPa
  • VPD planning value: 1.25 kPa
  • PPFD: 600–800 µmol/m²/s

Development

  • More visible flower sites.
  • Increasing resin production.
  • Stretch gradually slows down.
  • The plant invests more resources in flowers.

Care

  • Check regularly for moisture accumulating in foliage.
  • Adjust exhaust and circulation fans to the increasing leaf mass.
  • Regularly inspect flowers and leaves for pests and mold.
  • Avoid severe stress or major interventions.

11. Stage 7 – Mid flowering

Flowering weeks: 5–7

Goal

Healthy ongoing flower development with as little mold risk as possible.

Conditions

  • Day temperature: 22–26 °C
  • Night temperature: 18–22 °C
  • Relative humidity: 40–50%
  • VPD: 1.3–1.5 kPa
  • VPD planning value: 1.4 kPa
  • PPFD: 600–900 µmol/m²/s

Development

  • Inflorescences become larger and denser.
  • Resin production increases.
  • Smell may become stronger.
  • Water and nutrient requirements may change.

Care

  • Avoid high humidity.
  • Watch for moisture problems even inside dense flower structures.
  • Do not direct strong fans continuously onto flowers.
  • Watch for early signs of bud rot.
  • Adjust nutrient supply according to plant condition.
  • Avoid overfertilization.

Important: Mold prevention is increasingly important at this stage. VPD alone does not guarantee safe humidity.

12. Stage 8 – Late flowering and maturation

Flowering weeks: 8 until harvest; some plants need considerably longer.

Goal

Maturation under stable conditions with minimal mold risk.

Conditions

  • Day temperature: 21–26 °C
  • Night temperature: 18–21 °C
  • Relative humidity: 40–50%
  • VPD guidance range: 1.4–1.6 kPa
  • VPD planning value: 1.5 kPa
  • Lighting: Continue the established flowering cycle

Development

  • Flowers continue to mature.
  • New leaf growth usually slows.
  • Some older leaves may yellow naturally.
  • Trichomes change appearance.
  • Pistils progressively change color.

Care

  • Keep temperature and relative humidity stable.
  • Regularly check flowers for mold.
  • Avoid forcing unnecessary large climate changes.
  • Avoid late, heavy interventions.
  • Choose harvest timing according to actual maturity.

VPD caution

A theoretically higher VPD in late flowering is not a reason to dry the plant out unnecessarily.

Especially at lower temperatures, sensible humidity control is more important than rigidly forcing 1.5 kPa.

Continuously high VPD can cause water stress.

13. Daytime and nighttime climate

Day and night must be assessed separately when evaluating the climate.

During the light period

  • Keep VPD within the stage-specific target range.
  • Consider leaf-surface temperature.
  • Keep temperature and relative humidity as stable as possible.
  • Avoid sudden changes in light intensity.

During the dark period

  • A moderate temperature drop is acceptable.
  • Falling temperatures can raise relative humidity.
  • Avoid condensation on leaves and flowers.
  • Maintain circulation and adequate exhaust ventilation.
  • During flowering, avoid extended humidity periods above about 55–60%; denser flowers may call for lower values.

VPD at night

A single generally valid nighttime VPD target is not scientifically established.

Transpiration changes substantially in darkness. The difference between leaf and air temperatures may also change.

Therefore:

  1. Use daytime VPD as the primary physiological plant reference.
  2. Record and assess nighttime VPD separately.
  3. At night, focus especially on preventing excessive humidity and condensation.
  4. Do not automatically apply a daytime leaf-temperature offset at night without checking it.

Recommended data analysis

Record separately for each growing stage:

  • Average VPD with lights on
  • Average VPD with lights off
  • Minimum and maximum VPD
  • Average daytime/nighttime temperature
  • Average daytime/nighttime relative humidity
  • Proportion of the light period within the recommended VPD range

VPD means should be calculated from the individual calculated VPD measurements, not simply from average temperature and average relative humidity.

14. Watering and nutrients

One fixed water volume is not suitable for every plant. Demand depends on:

  • Pot size
  • Root development
  • Substrate
  • Temperature
  • Relative humidity and VPD
  • Light intensity
  • Plant mass
  • Growth stage

14.1 Watering principles

Seedling stage:

Small amounts; avoid keeping the entire pot permanently saturated.

Vegetative stage:

Watering volume and frequency increase as the roots develop.

Flowering stage:

Check water use regularly. Avoid severe dry periods and persistent waterlogging.

Late flowering:

Match watering to actual demand. There is no general need to completely dry the plant out before harvest.

14.2 Substrate-dependent pH guidance

Medium Typical pH range
Soil 6.0–7.0
Coco substrate 5.5–6.5
Hydroponics 5.5–6.5

These values are general guidance. The pH in the root zone is what matters; irrigation water, nutrient solution and runoff are not necessarily equivalent.

14.3 Nutrient supply

Vegetative:

Provide nitrogen and other major and trace nutrients according to need.

Flowering:

Nutrient demand changes as flowers develop. Adjust feeding plans to the growing medium and plant response.

Important:

  • Do not apply a universal EC or fertilizer dosage to all substrates.
  • Overfertilization can create symptoms similar to nutrient deficiencies.
  • Before making a correction, check water balance, pH, temperature and root condition.
  • A forced multiday flush immediately before harvest is not a universally proven necessity.

15. Determining harvest timing

Flowering week alone is not a reliable indicator of harvest readiness.

15.1 Observe trichomes

Use a suitable magnifier or microscope to inspect trichomes on flower calyxes.

Appearance Typical interpretation
Mostly clear Often not yet fully mature
Mostly milky Advanced ripeness
Milky with a few amber trichomes A commonly chosen harvest window
Increasing proportion of amber Further advanced maturation

The precise effect cannot be reliably predicted from trichome color alone. Genetics and chemical composition also matter.

Important: Trichomes on sugar leaves may mature before those on the flowers themselves. Whenever possible, assess the flower calyxes.

15.2 Additional signs of ripeness

  • Flower calyxes have visibly swollen.
  • Many pistils have changed color.
  • Rapid flower growth has slowed.
  • Trichome changes are monitored over several days.

15.3 Before harvest

  • Carefully check plants and flowers for mold.
  • Prepare a clean, preferably cool drying area.
  • Clean suitable tools.
  • Check air circulation and dehumidification in the drying area.

16. Drying

Drying is a separate post-harvest stage.

Typical duration: 7–14 days, depending on temperature, humidity, flower size and airflow.

16.1 Climate reference

Parameter Recommendation
Temperature 15–20 °C
Relative humidity 50–60%
Lighting Dark
Air movement Gentle and indirect
Exhaust ventilation As needed, regularly
VPD regulation Do not use a plant-VPD target

Lower temperatures support gentle drying. Persistently high humidity, however, increases the risk of microbial contamination.

16.2 Why is there no plant VPD during drying?

The harvested plant is no longer connected to an active root system.

Water loss during drying occurs from the plant material under different conditions from normal transpiration in a living plant.

Temperature, relative humidity, air exchange and drying progress should therefore be the primary control variables.

A physical vapor-pressure gradient still exists, but it is not used as a normal leaf-VPD target for growth control.

16.3 Procedure

  1. Harvest the plant parts and discard visibly damaged or suspicious material.
  2. Hang larger branches with adequate spacing or place individual flowers on suitable drying racks.
  3. Maintain darkness.
  4. Keep temperature and relative humidity stable.
  5. Ensure gentle airflow without blowing directly on the flowers.
  6. Check daily for mold, condensation and drying progress.
  7. Move to storage or curing only after sufficient drying.

16.4 When is drying complete?

Possible indicators:

  • Small twigs start to snap rather than bend.
  • The surface of the flowers no longer feels moist.
  • Flowers are dry outside without remaining noticeably wet inside.
  • Moisture stabilizes after brief storage in a suitable measuring container.

The twig-snap test is only a rough indicator. Water-activity or equilibrium relative-humidity measurement allows more accurate assessment.

16.5 Drying mistakes

Temperature too high:

Drying accelerates and sensitive volatile compounds may be lost more readily.

Humidity too low:

The outer layer may dry too quickly while the inside remains moist.

Humidity too high:

Increased risk of mold and microbial spoilage.

Insufficient air circulation:

Humid air can stagnate between tightly packed flowers.

Strong direct airflow:

May cause uneven, overly rapid surface drying.

Do not consume visibly moldy material or assume it is safe after merely removing affected spots.

17. Optional – Curing

A controlled curing stage may follow the initial drying process.

Typical duration: About 2–4 weeks or longer.

Conditions

  • Clean, food-safe containers
  • Dark and preferably cool storage
  • Hygrometer for monitoring
  • Frequently targeted equilibrium relative humidity inside the container: approximately 58–62%

Procedure

  1. Place only sufficiently pre-dried material in the containers.
  2. Do not overfill containers.
  3. Check relative humidity after closing.
  4. If moisture is too high, remove the material and continue drying.
  5. Check regularly for moisture and mold during the initial period.
  6. Ensure hygienic storage.

Persistently high moisture in sealed containers is problematic. Material with visible mold must be discarded.

A humidity-control pack in the container cannot replace adequate initial drying.

18. Typical problems and possible causes

Symptom Possible cause What to check
Seedling grows slowly Wet substrate, too little light, temperature Roots, moisture, PPFD
Leaves droop Over- or underwatering Substrate condition and pot weight
Burned leaf tips Overfertilization or light stress Feeding, pH, PPFD
Dry leaf edges High VPD, lack of water, heat VPD, roots, temperature
Very dark leaves Possible nutrient imbalance Feeding, pH, plant response
Large internodal spacing Genetics, light or stretch Light intensity and plant stage
Very slow flower development Genetics, light, temperature or stress Climate and plant condition
Unusual moisture in flowers Insufficient air circulation Relative humidity, spacing
Suspected mold High humidity, condensation, dense flowers Inspect contamination, discard affected material

Note: These symptoms are not definitive. Several causes may occur together. A single leaf change should not trigger drastic interventions without further checks.

General priorities

  1. Healthy roots and sufficient watering according to need
  2. Appropriate temperature and lighting
  3. Controlled relative humidity
  4. Stage-dependent VPD
  5. Good air circulation and mold prevention
  6. Appropriate nutrient supply
  7. Regular observation of plant development

A stable environment is generally more useful than frequent drastic changes to individual targets.
